# Layout and Composition

## Visual mass

Mass comes from size, contrast, saturation, density, edge strength, elevation, motion, and isolation. A small bright button can outweigh a large muted panel. Judge mass, not only dimensions.

## Alignment systems

Use deliberate alignment lines across headings, inputs, tables, cards, sidebars, and actions. Inconsistency that is not expressive intent reads as drift.

## Density

Repeated-use operational tools often need more density than marketing pages. Do not inflate rows, cards, and gaps to look premium. Conversely, dense expert tools still need grouping and readable hit targets.

## Asymmetry

Asymmetry is valuable when it supports hierarchy or identity. Random misalignment is not editorial tension.

## Empty space

Empty space can create focus, pacing, or hierarchy. Do not fill every blank area with slogans, badges, fake metrics, or illustration. But distinguish intentional breathing room from a layout that wastes half the viewport because components were designed in isolation.

## Fixed vs scrolling regions

For queues/editors/dashboards, decide which regions remain stable while content scrolls. Persistent primary actions should not disappear accidentally. Avoid nested scroll regions unless each has a clear ownership and input behavior.
